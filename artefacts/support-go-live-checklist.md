# Before a bot answers your customers: the go-live checklist

The model is rarely the problem. The document it reads from is.

- [ ] One source of truth outside the bot: returns, refunds, pricing, promos, delivery, hours. One document. One owner. A "last reviewed" date at the top. No policy lives in a prompt or in someone's head.
- [ ] 20 test questions written by you, including five nasty ones ("can I return it after 45 days if I only opened the box?"). Run them before the bot sees a customer. Keep the answers.
- [ ] A rule for what the bot may not answer alone: anything with money, dates or exceptions goes to a human draft first.
- [ ] Drafts, not sends: the bot writes, a person approves with one tap, for the first two weeks at least.
- [ ] A weekly sample: read 10 real conversations every Friday. Look for: cited the current policy? asked for the order number? invented a discount?
- [ ] When the document changes, re-run the 20 questions before the bot goes back on.
- [ ] Cost line written down: the tool's monthly price and the hours it must save to be worth it. Re-check in 30 days.

If you cannot tick the first box, do not go live. Start with saved replies for the 15 questions that are 80 % of your inbox.
