## 1. Process · stage 1 · M2 Teardown

**Reference:** [hassandotai](https://instagram.com/reel/DcmK70aO5VP), 16 days old · stage 1, best in block Process

### What they shot and what it did

**Topic:** Three back-office automations, sold as a way to make $8,000 a month building them for clients.
**What the reel shows:** A 52-second listicle at a whiteboard. The hook puts a money figure and a timeframe first, then second 5 removes the two objections that disqualify most viewers (you do not have to code, you do not have to show your face), and only then come the three automations, one per named industry: a law firm intake that collects new client details, sorts the documents and sends everything to the right person; a recruitment flow that reads resumes and forwards the best candidates; an insurance flow that collects customer documents, checks what is missing and updates the system. Two workflow diagrams on a black background carry the proof slot. The close gates everything behind commenting one word.
**Why it worked for them:** The three examples are ordinary document handling, which is why a buyer can picture them, and the reel says so out loud at second 37: these are the ones businesses actually understand. The objections are cleared before any content, so nobody drops out early. The specifics stay behind the comment gate, which is what produced the highest saves in the batch.
**Result:** 21 620 plays · 1.9× the author's own norm · 40.29 shares and 62.07 saves per thousand
**Does not transfer:** The $8,000 month is asserted with nothing behind it, and we do not make income claims. The whole frame is build-it-and-sell-it to clients, an agency pitch rather than a process the viewer owns. Resume filtering is out: candidate screening is the one topic this audience rejected both times it came up. And the detail sits behind the comment gate, so the viewer leaves with a shopping list, not a process.

### Our version

**Topic:** The same job details get typed three times before the work starts
**Angle:** They sold intake as a product to build for somebody else. We take the same document intake and hand it to the person who already runs it, at the level where it actually breaks: the details arrive by voicemail, text and a photo of a note, and one person carries them into the calendar and onto the job sheet from memory. The machine fills a draft, the owner taps approve, and the job sheet stops depending on who is off this week. We say out loud what stays human, which the original never does.
**For whom:** The owner of a small trades company who takes jobs on the phone while he is on site, two vans and a part-time office helper (persona Rick).
**Process:** New job intake: a lead arrives by voicemail, text or a photo of a handwritten note, and somebody turns it into a calendar entry and a job sheet with the name, address, access details and what the customer asked for.
**Friction:** The same details are retyped in two or three places, the access details and customer exceptions live in one person's head, and when that person is on a roof or off sick the job sheet is wrong or late.
**AI does / human keeps:** The machine reads the voicemail and the photo and fills a draft job sheet with the fields it can find. The owner approves it before anything is scheduled, prices are read from the price sheet and never written by the machine, and nothing goes to the customer automatically.
**Next action for the viewer:** Write the intake down on one page before touching any tool: the process, its owner, what starts it, what finished looks like, and the exceptions you keep in your head.
**Why someone forwards it:** It ends with a page anyone can fill in tonight, and it names the part that has to stay human instead of promising the whole job goes away.
**Format:** M2 Teardown: A repeated process with a named owner, three inputs, a friction point, a boundary and a risk when it goes wrong. That is the Teardown definition in POSITIONING section 8, and it is the default for the Process block.

**Friction:** Nobody owns the subscriptions, nobody can name the job each one does, and nobody has counted how often that job actually runs, so the decision to keep or cancel is made on a feeling about the price.

**Next action for the viewer:** Take one subscription, count the runs by hand for two real weeks, then divide the monthly price by the runs and compare it with what the job costs by hand.
**Why someone forwards it:** It carries a number somebody else paid: our own ten-line ledger, 889 requests at two cents, $17.78, itemised. And it gives the receiver a sum to run on their own card tonight.
**Format:** M2 Radar: A cost signal turned into one work decision, keep it or cancel it, with the limitation named. That is the Radar definition and the default for the Money block.

### Our version

**Next action for the viewer:** List every AI thing you pay for with four columns: the job it does, who is paged when it breaks, where the knowledge it reads lives, and a review date. Anything whose knowledge only lives inside the vendor goes on the list first.
**Why someone forwards it:** It hands the receiver one question they can ask in the next vendor call, and it is the rare AI video that tells you what to cancel.
**Format:** M2 Radar: The subject forces it.
