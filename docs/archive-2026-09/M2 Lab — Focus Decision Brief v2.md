## 1. Proposed decision

### Public positioning

> **M2 Lab helps AI-curious SME owner-operators understand what AI can realistically do in their daily work — then turn one repetitive task into a useful workflow, without coding or chasing every new tool.**

### Editorial learning promise

> **Understand what matters. Choose one task. Build one useful workflow.**

### What this is

Education through practical application. M2 Lab explains AI capability, tool choice and limits **only in relation to a real work task**. The promised outcome is not “know AI”; it is a first useful workflow.

### What this is not

- AI news, tool reviews or generic “best tools” lists;
- content promising income, fully automated businesses or hundreds of agents;
- a course, agency or proven client-implementation service before evidence exists;
- content for developers or advanced AI builders.

---

## 2. The one reader

**Primary reader:** an English-speaking or English-working owner-operator of a roughly 2–50-person service SME in Europe. They still own or oversee marketing, sales, operations or client service. They have no AI team, know that AI exists, and do not know which repeated task to improve first.

**Adjacent reader:** a non-technical functional lead in the same type of business who owns a repeatable process and can test one workflow.

**Reader job-to-be-done:**

> “Help me understand whether AI is useful for this part of my work, choose a sensible first task, and avoid wasting time or money on the wrong tool.”

**Test for every post:**

> Would this person recognise their work, learn one concrete thing, and know the first next step?

## 3. Three content blocks — decide now

These are **production formats**, not the same thing as the themes in section 6. Every Reel belongs to one block. Do not add a fourth format during the first 12-post cycle.

| Block | Job | A post must contain | Target action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M2 Radar** | Translate a tool launch, market signal or external example into one work decision | A named task and reader. It cannot be news or a tool review alone. | Share / reach |
| **M2 Builds** | Show a workflow you actually built or tested | The process, result and what broke, cost more than expected or still needs human review. | Follow / save |
| **M2 Teardown** | Break down a repeated business process before proposing AI | Where time, money or risk leaks; what AI can take; where a human must remain. | Save / search / share |

**Discussion rule:** keep these three blocks as the only formats for the first cycle. A new idea must fit one of them; do not create a fourth block to avoid choosing.

**Current theme mapping:**

- **Start with the task**, **Workflow economics** and **Safe adoption** usually become **M2 Teardowns**.
- **Build log: what broke** becomes **M2 Builds**.
- **Tool -> work change** becomes **M2 Radar**.

The exact 12-post split across the blocks is a future production decision. The rule now is that each post gets one block.

## 4. Geography: what we do now

**Do not put a country in the public positioning for the first 12–24 posts.** The account is English-language; the reader is defined by their job and AI maturity, not by a country label.

Instagram organic recommendations are primarily based on the viewer’s interests, interactions with similar content and predicted actions. An ordinary Reel cannot be reliably targeted to one country by the creator. Geography becomes useful only with a real local case, collaborator, market norm, event, price or regulation. [Meta on unconnected recommendations](https://ai.meta.com/blog/ai-unconnected-content-recommendations-facebook-instagram/), [Instagram Feed system card](https://ai.meta.com/tools/system-cards/instagram-feed-ranking/)

**Commercial research hypotheses, not account identities:**

| Hypothesis | Role now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Netherlands** | First research / collaboration hypothesis | Strong English accessibility, but target international or cross-border SMEs — not all Dutch local businesses |
| **Ireland** | First English-first market hypothesis | Clean language fit, but smaller market |
| **UK** | Distribution and later-test market | Large but consultant’s saturation warning makes it a poor default identity |
| **Denmark / Sweden** | Later test | English-working and digitally mature; basic AI education may have less whitespace |

**Rule:** first collect evidence of the countries that actually follow, save, share, comment and DM. Only then run a 3–4-post local series for one country.

## 5. What your own radar says

### Evidence base

- Current database: **2,363 reels from 100 English-language accounts**, snapshot dated 1 September 2026.
- Existing top-card concepts already point toward cost, failures, adoption risk and one concrete process — not general tool news.
- In the analysed top hundred, shares were a stronger separator of winners than saves; the production model favours specific, transferable claims over empty reach.

### What the evidence supports

| What performed as a mechanism | What M2 Lab takes from it | What M2 Lab refuses to copy |
|---|---|---|
| One process with a visible outcome | One task -> one workflow -> one decision | “AI runs your whole business” |
| A number, cost or contradiction | Hours, cost, risk, failure point | Fake ROI or income claims |
| A build with a result | Show the system, the test and the break | Demoing someone else’s tool as if it were proof |
| A strong tool/news hook | Translate it into a business task | “New model released” as the entire story |

### What the evidence does **not** prove

The radar analyses AI-content competitors, not M2 Lab’s future English-working European SME audience. It helps choose ideas and production mechanics; it cannot prove that a topic will convert your reader. Your own first 12 posts are the test.

### Existing M2 proof available now

- The content radar itself: it ranked a 654-view reel above a 25,000-view reel before you fixed the logic.
- The real collection cost: 623 units / $12.46, including an avoidable spend because a free source existed.
- Concrete failures: sources closed, video links expired, and written rules were not enforced in code.

This is credible material for build and teardown posts. It is finite; do not pretend it is a client case study.

## 6. First 12-post thematic test

This is a thematic slate, **not scripts yet**. Each row becomes a repeatable series, not a one-off topic.

| Series | Posts | What the reader gets | Example title direction | Target action |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **Start with the task** | 3 | A way to select a first AI use case | “Don’t start with an AI tool. Start with this 10-minute task map.” | Save / share |
| **Build log: what broke** | 3 | Honest proof that you tested a workflow | “We gave an agent 2,363 Reels. Here is the decision it got wrong.” | Follow / trust |
| **Workflow economics** | 2 | A way to judge cost, time and trade-offs | “Free AI has a price. Here is the bill that arrives in week three.” | Share / save |
| **Safe adoption** | 2 | A clear boundary for data, clients and human review | “Your team turned on AI in Gmail. Here is what it can read.” | Save / share |
| **Tool -> work change** | 2 | A current tool update translated into one practical decision | “A new voice agent is not a customer-service system. Start with these calls.” | Reach / share |

### Theme rules

1. One Reel = one repeated task, not three tips.
2. Each build must be genuinely tested by Misha and/or Max and include a failure, limitation or human-review point.
3. A news/tool story passes only when it changes a recognisable process.
4. “Best AI tools for small business”, free lead-scraping and automated-income claims fail the positioning filter.
5. Use English. Do not force a country label into a Reel without real local substance.

### Proven source material already in the archive

- “You’re paying for the same document twice. Most teams never look.”
- “Free Claude Code has a price. It just isn’t money.”
- “They run 137 agents. We ran one. Here’s the step where it broke.”
- “Every missed call has a price. Here is how to work out yours.”
- “A good-looking dashboard is not a system. Unplug it and see.”
- “Same 2,400 companies. One cost nine dollars, the other cost nothing.”

These are raw directions. Publish only after the claim, process and evidence are checked.
