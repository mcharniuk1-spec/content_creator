# 02. Audience

## The core audience, as a job to be done

The person responsible for figuring out where AI should actually fit in a small
business. This is one job to be done, not one job title. It may be an owner or CEO
in a small company, an operations lead, a team lead who owns a recurring process,
or a functional manager asked to "figure out AI" for the team.

Write to that job, never to a title, and never to a crowd.

## The three roles in an engagement

| Role | Who they are | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Owner or CEO | Clarity, lower chaos, and a team that can use AI responsibly. |
| Champion | Operations or team lead | A clear first process and a realistic implementation path. |
| User | The person doing the repeated task | A workflow that makes work easier and keeps their judgment. |

A Reel usually speaks to the Champion, gets forwarded to the Buyer, and has to
survive being read by the User. Write so all three can follow it.

## Audience level: 0 to 6 out of 10

- They know the main AI tools and may have tried ChatGPT or Claude.
- They have no repeatable way of using them at work.
- They are open to practical no-code or low-code workflows.
- They need plain language and real examples.

Assume they know what a chatbot is. Assume they do not know what an API, a webhook,
an embedding or an agent framework is. Define any term the moment it appears.

## Who we do not serve

Advanced technical builders who want local models, deep orchestration, custom
infrastructure or multi-agent architecture. When a topic only interests that group,
it belongs in the workshop channel or nowhere.

## What they know, do not know, and fear

**They know:** AI matters, competitors are experimenting, their team is already
pasting work into chatbots without a rule, and their days are full of repeated tasks.

**They do not know:** which task to start with, which tools are worth attention,
what output can be trusted, how to introduce AI without creating more chaos, and
what a first working version actually looks like.

**They fear:** wasting money on a tool nobody uses, an AI mistake reaching a
customer, their data leaking, looking foolish in front of the team, and being sold
a transformation they cannot maintain.

Speak to the fear by naming the human review step, every time.

## The four questions they bring

| Question | Answered by |
|---|---|
| "Is this solution relevant to me?" | M2 Radar |
| "What does a first working version look like?" | M2 Builds |
| "Is this process suitable for AI, and how would it work?" | M2 Teardowns |
| "So what do I do next?" | The closing line of every publication |

The fourth question is never optional. If a piece does not answer it, it is not
finished.
