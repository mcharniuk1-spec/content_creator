# M2 Lab — positioning

**This file is meant to change.** It is the one place where our angle lives, and the agent
reads it on every run to write the angle, the hook and the shot plan for each card. When our
positioning moves, edit here — nothing else needs touching.

Version 1, 3 September 2026. Written from decisions made while building the radar, not
from a branding exercise.

---

## 1. Who we are

Two people putting AI into real business processes and showing what actually happens —
including the parts that break.

**Misha** — economics of adoption, processes, what a workflow costs and what it saves.
**Max** — the technical side: builds, stacks, where the tooling gives out.

We are not a media outlet and not a course. We are practitioners publishing our own work.

## 2. What we talk about

**AI inside a working process.** Not "AI news", not "AI tools", not "how to make money with
AI" — those are neighbouring niches with their own crowds. Our subject is the step between
"there is a model" and "the work changed": what it replaced, what it cost, where it failed.

The single question every reel has to answer: **what changes in someone's Monday because
of this?**

## 3. Who it is for

Operators and founders of small teams who already run the process themselves — sales,
support, research, operations — and are deciding whether to hand a piece of it to a machine.
They are not beginners and not engineers. They can read a number and they can smell a pitch.

**We do not name the audience on screen.** A deliberate choice with a known cost: an
unfamiliar viewer decides in a second whether the reel is for them, and irrelevant reach
hurts distribution. Revisit once our own numbers say something.

## 4. Three formats

Every week the radar picks separately in each. A general top would turn the account into
AI news within a month.

### M2 Radar — 2 slots a week
New model, tool, trend, viral demo. **Passes only if the topic survives another week** —
there are three to seven days between the plan and the shoot, and a one-day story dies first.
Disqualified: benchmarks, review for review's sake, anything with no exit into a work process.
Our angle is never "this came out" but "this changes the maths for exactly one kind of team".
Target signal: **share**.

### M2 Builds — 2 slots a week
Builds, tests, comparisons. **Passes only if we can actually build it and something in it
breaks.** Without the break there is no Builds — there is an ad for someone else's product.
Disqualified: anything not reproducible in a day of prep; a clean demo of a tool we did not
touch. Our angle: "we put it into a real process — here is what survived".
Target signal: **save and follow**.

### M2 Teardown — 1 slot a week, 2 every second week
A topic that repeated across several authors, not one that spiked once. **Two conditions,
both required:** there is a concrete process — ours, an industry-wide one, or one a viewer
sent — and there is a reason to forward it: a number, a contradiction, "here is what this
costs you per week", "here is where AI has no business being".
Our angle: process step by step → where time and money leak → what can be handed over →
where it must not be → limits and risks.
Target signal: **save and search**. The title and the first caption line are written as a
search query.

## 5. What we never publish

- **Money-from-AI content.** "Make $10k with AI agents" is a neighbouring niche. We can
  study its mechanics; we never take its topic.
- **General AI education and news for a wide audience.** Explaining what a transformer is,
  or that a new model shipped, without a process behind it.
- **Someone else's characters, cloned voices, generated scenes with real people.** This one
  is not taste, it is a rule: it is invisible in the numbers and obvious in the frames.
- **Claims with no proof we own.** No case studies we did not run, no client logos we do not
  have, no testimonials.
- **Anything not in English.**

## 6. How a reel is built

Derived from analysing the top hundred reels in the niche, not invented.

**Fixed:** one location and light · a banner in one place, one typeface, held for the whole
reel · one caption style · the screen shown as a full-frame insert · **50–70 seconds** · one
closing line.

**Variable:** who is on camera and how many — one, both, or Misha first and Max after.

**A change of speaker is the only cut we allow.** Everything else is shot in one take.
Ground: of the top hundred, 44 have effectively no cuts and 12 have exactly zero, and the
difference in signal against edited ones is inside the noise.

**Why 50–70 seconds:** the winners' median is 55 seconds against 47 for the rest. Under
twenty seconds is 5% of winners and 13% of the rest.

**The question to ask of every reel: why would anyone forward this?** A share separates a
winner 3.6× more reliably than anything else; a save, 2.8×.

**In a process reel we never name our own service.** The median for that topic in the niche
is 22 shares per thousand; the ceiling is 2 755. The difference is exactly this.

## 7. Proof we can actually show

An angle is only as strong as the evidence behind it. What we own today:

- **The radar itself** — a working tool built in two days: what it cost, where it lied, what
  we put in to stop it. Our own ranker put a 654-view reel above a 25 000-view one.
- **Our own bill** — 623 units, $12.46, and the line we overpaid because a free source
  existed and we did not know.
- **Concrete failures** — the free profile source that closed mid-run; video links that die
  in hours; a rule written in our own documents that the code did not enforce for two days.
- **Numbers from the niche** — 2 352 reels, a hundred taken apart frame by frame, 89
  transcripts. Every claim above traces to them.

**This stock runs out in ten to fifteen reels.** After that the proof has to come from what
we actually implement — our own processes and, later, client ones. When an angle has no
evidence behind it, it is a take, and takes are what everyone else publishes.

## 8. Tone

Plain, specific, unhurried. A number instead of an adjective. We name the price and the
failure before the win — that is the whole reason to listen to us rather than to someone
with a better camera.

No hype openers, no "this changes everything", no countdowns to a reveal. If the first line
is not true on its own, it is not a hook, it is bait.

## 9. Who leads what

Assigned by topic, not by turn. Economics and processes — Misha. Technique and builds — Max.
Either can carry a Teardown; the speaker change is the one edit we allow, so both in one reel
is normal.
