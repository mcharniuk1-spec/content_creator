# Research and Analysis Contract

## Objective

Produce a single source-grounded research package covering relevant public videos, static posts, captions, threads, ads, publications, and channel conventions for ArchFlow onboarding/knowledge reliability and hotel optimization content.

“All social media” means an explicit channel coverage matrix, not an unbounded promise. Every channel is `READY`, `PARTIAL`, `BLOCKED`, or `NOT_REQUESTED`, with a dated capability receipt and active backend.

## Required master files

The first research run must produce:

1. `research/master-social-analysis.md` — one synthesized file covering channels, themes, formats, audience language, evidence, caveats, and original content implications.
2. `research/video/video-link-ledger.csv` — every discovered/admitted video URL and metadata.
3. `research/material-link-ledger.csv` — every admitted photo, carousel, thread, X post, LinkedIn post/article, ad, publication, landing page, or other material.
4. one structured analysis artifact per admitted video.
5. one structured analysis artifact per admitted static/text unit or a batch file when the units are homogeneous.
6. a channel coverage receipt and a source-rights ledger.

## Collection sequence

1. Run Agent Reach doctor and save a sanitized receipt.
2. Define target topics, audiences, date window, languages, geography, channel priority, and minimum evidence depth.
3. Define source allowlist and exclusion list.
4. Search official sources, public pages, RSS, and verified active Agent Reach backends.
5. Use Sherlock only for owner-approved public brand/product username candidates.
6. Add every candidate URL to the relevant ledger before analysis.
7. Resolve duplicates and canonical URLs.
8. Record accessibility, collection route, published/observed dates, rights state, and whether transcript/image extraction is allowed.
9. Analyze only admitted materials.
10. Independently review the evidence and coverage gaps before creative work.

Search snippets, thumbnails, popularity counts, ad-library presence, and repeated wording are discovery signals. They are not proof of business demand, performance, purchase intent, or causality.

## Platform segmentation

### LinkedIn

Analyze job-to-be-done framing, role language, problem specificity, post structures, carousels/documents, article depth, proof style, comment questions, and CTA patterns. Use public access only. Company/creator observations are context, not endorsement or performance proof.

### X and Threads

Analyze thread architecture, opening claim, compression, reply/quote context, objection handling, visual attachment role, and CTA. Preserve full conversational context and dates. If no active backend exists, mark coverage blocked rather than simulating it from search snippets.

### Reels, Instagram, and TikTok

Analyze hook seconds, talking-head/B-roll ratio, overlay density, beat duration, resets, visual demonstrations, audio function, captions, and ending. Logged-in research, cookie reuse, bulk scraping, and downloads require separate approval.

### YouTube and Shorts

Use verified metadata and transcript routes. Analyze title/thumbnail promise, intro, scene timing, explanation arc, evidence, screen capture, voice, B-roll, CTA, comments only when accessible, and short-form extraction opportunities.

### Ad libraries

Record advertiser/page, platform/library, active date, format, framing, proof unit, offer, CTA, and observable variants. Never infer spend, conversions, audience targeting, or winner status unless the library directly provides it and the claim is properly scoped.

### Publication library

Prioritize official documentation, research, trusted industry sources, product changelogs, public case studies, and timely reporting. Classify facts as stable, time-sensitive, or highly volatile and retrieve fresh evidence accordingly.

## Video analysis layers

Every admitted video needs:

- identity and provenance: canonical URL, platform, creator/account class, dates, duration, language, rights state;
- text: transcript with timecodes, on-screen text, caption, title, description, CTA;
- semantic map: topic, audience, problem, pain, promise, mechanism, proof, objections, resolution;
- temporal map: hook, beats, scene/shot intervals, information resets, CTA, silence;
- visual map: talking head, product UI, screen recording, photo, graphic, generated image, B-roll, chart, typography, camera, crop, motion;
- layer map: base plate, person, foreground, overlay, captions, annotations, logo, UI, effects;
- audio map: speech, music, ambience, transitions, SFX, loudness role, rights;
- production abstraction: which techniques are reusable without copying expression;
- regeneration map: separate assets, frame prompts, transitions, screen-capture needs, audio stems, montage steps;
- originality exclusions: words, sequence, composition, identity, music, and metaphors that must not be imitated;
- evidence/reviewer status.

## Static and text analysis layers

Every admitted static unit needs:

- platform, format, dimensions/aspect, slide/frame count, date, author/account class, rights state;
- exact visible text or a bounded transcription;
- reading order, headline/promise, proof, objection, CTA;
- grid, margins, composition, focal point, crop, whitespace, safe zones;
- typography roles and approximate density, not unauthorized font copying;
- layer plan: background, subject/product, chart/UI, shapes, icons, text, annotations, branding;
- color/contrast/accessibility;
- engagement or popularity signals only when directly observed and properly scoped;
- editable reconstruction plan in Figma;
- original adaptation rules and forbidden distinctive elements.

## Evidence taxonomy

- `FACT`: directly supported and accurately scoped.
- `INTERPRETATION`: reasoned meaning tied to listed evidence.
- `HYPOTHESIS`: testable creative or market proposition.
- `GAP`: missing, blocked, stale, conflicting, or unverifiable information.

Each insight must list its evidence IDs. Any factual public claim proposed for content must pass a claim review separate from creative preference.

## Clean-room originality

Use references to abstract techniques: hook class, beat length, proof structure, framing family, layer stack, transition family, and audience question. Do not reproduce a source's script, distinctive phrase, narrative order, branded UI, recognizable character, exact composition, music, voice, or identity. Maintain a similarity-risk note for every derived concept.

## Done conditions

- ledgers contain canonical URLs and no unregistered analyzed materials;
- every channel has a truthful capability state;
- every primary fact has a direct evidence parent and date;
- every analysis separates observation from inference;
- source and rights status are explicit;
- blocked channels remain visibly blocked;
- a reviewer validates coverage and originality before the creative pod starts.
