# M2 Lab — Marketing vs Sales vs Finance

**Purpose:** decide which horizontal business function can become M2 Lab's first editorial lane.  
**Status:** discussion draft, 4 September 2026.  
**Important:** this is a decision about content focus, not a claim that M2 Lab can already implement every workflow as a service.

---

## Executive conclusion

- **Marketing** is the easiest lane for content production and visual demonstrations. It is also the most crowded and the easiest place to become generic “AI tips”.
- **Sales operations** is the strongest first lane if the goal is to attract future service clients: the owner can connect the pain directly to lost revenue, while the workflows are concrete and repeatable.
- **Finance administration** has the sharpest owner pain and less generic competition, but should not be the primary lane until M2 has stronger domain evidence. It carries privacy, accuracy, tax and trust boundaries.

**Recommended test:** make **Sales operations** the primary 12-post lens. Use **marketing** only where it feeds the sales process, and use **finance administration** as a later secondary series about invoices, receivables and cash visibility — never as accounting or tax advice.

Candidate wording, not a replacement for the approved broad positioning:

> M2 Lab shows owner-led service SMEs how to turn lost enquiries, inconsistent follow-up and manual proposals into one useful AI-assisted sales workflow.

---

## What the evidence says

| Evidence | What it means for the decision |
|---|---|
| In OECD's 2024 survey of more than 5,000 SMEs in Austria, Canada, Germany, Ireland, Japan, Korea and the UK, marketing and sales were the most common business-support application of GenAI. Finance and legal were among the least common. | Marketing/sales has lower explanation friction; finance has more whitespace but a higher adoption and trust hurdle. |
| Eurostat's 2025 enterprise survey reports that, among EU enterprises using AI, 34.1% of small firms (10–49 staff) use it for marketing or sales, while 22.2% use it for accounting, controlling or finance management. | The gap is meaningful but not an argument that finance lacks value; it tells us the entry point is harder. |
| UK DSIT research among businesses using or planning AI found planned/current application in marketing (72%), sales (49%) and finance (29%). | Sales is a recognisable middle ground: more commercially direct than marketing, but less trust-sensitive than finance. |
| In UK SME research, owners' near-term priorities included increasing sales (57%) and improving cash flow (47%). The most cited reasons to adopt technology were saving money (69%), improving quality (62%) and saving working time (62%). | Content should lead with a concrete operational pain and a credible process change — not “use this AI tool”. |
| Across OECD SME evidence, skill/know-how is a major adoption barrier. | M2's educational angle is useful: explain the task, choice and human boundary before teaching a tool. |

**Scope note:** Eurostat covers enterprises with 10+ people; it is not a direct measurement of microbusinesses. The UK functional percentages are among businesses already using or planning AI, not every UK business.

## 1. Marketing operations

### What can be AI-assisted

- Researching audience questions, reviews, competitor claims and market language.
- Turning one source idea into a brief, carousel, email, landing-page outline and sales enablement asset.
- Drafting first versions of campaign copy, subject lines, ad variations and customer-segment messages.
- Building content and campaign calendars from known business priorities.
- Summarising campaign results and finding a testable next question.
- Maintaining CRM segments and lead-nurture drafts, once the underlying customer data is clean.

### What must stay human

- Positioning, brand judgment and the final claim.
- Approval of public content and paid advertising.
- The decision about which segment matters and what not to say.
- Measuring commercial impact rather than mistaking output volume for growth.

### Owner pain -> useful promise

| Business context | Pain an owner recognises | Content / workflow promise |
|---|---|---|
| B2B agency or consultancy | “We publish, but content is disconnected from the buyers we want.” | Turn calls, objections and wins into a repeatable research -> content -> nurture workflow. |
| Founder-led service business | “Everything waits for me to write or approve it.” | Build a system that turns one founder input into approved drafts without replacing judgment. |
| Local service business | “Demand is uneven and we do not know which messages bring enquiries.” | Turn reviews, enquiries and seasonal questions into a small campaign and follow-up system. |
| E-commerce | “The catalogue and customer questions grow faster than the team.” | Create product-information and lifecycle-message drafts with strict review points. |

### Why choose it

**Pros**

- Easy to demonstrate visually on Instagram.
- Many public examples and easy builds.
- M2 already has authentic proof from its content radar and research workflow.
- Lower consequence when a draft is wrong, if it is reviewed before publication.

**Cons**

- The noisiest AI-content category; generic tool lists will make M2 indistinguishable.
- “More content” is not a business outcome. Owners may not connect it to money.
- Easy to drift into creator education instead of SME operations.

**Strong content angles**

- “Your content calendar is not a marketing system if it never reaches a sales conversation.”
- “Stop asking AI for 30 post ideas. Start with the five objections your buyers repeat.”
- “We turned 23 sales-call notes into a content backlog. Here is the human step we did not automate.”

## 2. Sales operations

### What can be AI-assisted

- Capturing and routing inbound leads from forms, email, calls and DMs.
- Preparing lead research and qualification briefs from approved sources.
- Summarising calls into CRM fields, next actions and risks.
- Drafting personalised follow-ups from a call summary and a pre-approved offer library.
- Preparing first-draft proposals, scopes and meeting agendas.
- Flagging stale deals, missing next steps and repeated objections.
- Building a weekly pipeline summary for the owner.

### What must stay human

- Lead qualification criteria, pricing and deal strategy.
- Relationship building, negotiation and promises made to a client.
- Final proposal approval and sending.
- Consent, CRM hygiene and privacy controls.

### Owner pain -> useful promise

| Business context | Pain an owner recognises | Content / workflow promise |
|---|---|---|
| Agency / consultancy | “We win calls, then take days to follow up or build the proposal.” | Call -> CRM -> tailored follow-up -> proposal outline, with the owner approving the commercial decision. |
| B2B service SME | “Leads come in, but no one reliably owns the next step.” | One intake and follow-up workflow that makes lead ownership visible. |
| Local service / field service | “We miss calls and WhatsApp messages while doing the actual work.” | Turn missed enquiries into a structured callback queue, not an autonomous sales bot. |
| Recruitment / professional services | “Our team repeats discovery and proposal preparation every week.” | Turn discovery notes into a consistent brief, scope checklist and next-step draft. |

### Why choose it

**Pros**

- Pain connects directly to the owner's revenue priority.
- Clear before/after workflows: missed inquiry -> owned next step; call -> CRM; proposal prep -> review.
- Natural path to a future service offer.
- Enough variation for Radar, Builds and Teardowns without losing focus.

**Cons**

- Bad CRM data and unclear ownership will block automation.
- Can accidentally become spammy “lead-gen automation” content; M2 must stay on process quality and human review.
- Proof needs realistic systems and tests, not claims that AI closes deals.

**Strong content angles**

- “Your CRM does not have a lead problem. It has an unowned-next-step problem.”
- “The workflow is not ‘AI writes follow-ups’. It is ‘a client never waits because no one captured the next action’.”
- “We tested a call-to-proposal workflow. The draft was fast; the pricing decision still belonged to the owner.”

## 3. Finance administration

### What can be AI-assisted

- Extracting fields from invoices and receipts into a review queue.
- Chasing missing documents and preparing payment-reminder drafts.
- Keeping a receivables list and highlighting invoices that need a human decision.
- Preparing a weekly cash-in / cash-out summary from approved data.
- Comparing actual spend with a budget and preparing questions about anomalies.
- Producing a management-report draft from existing accounting or ERP outputs.

### What must stay human

- Tax treatment, accounting entries and statutory filings.
- Bank payments and approval of financial transactions.
- Final categorisation, reconciliations and audit evidence.
- Financial advice, credit decisions and forecasts presented as certainty.
- Data access, retention and vendor approval.

### Owner pain -> useful promise

| Business context | Pain an owner recognises | Content / workflow promise |
|---|---|---|
| Agency / consultancy | “We are busy, but I cannot tell what is due, overdue or profitable this month.” | Create a weekly owner view of invoices, receivables and cash questions — not a replacement for the accountant. |
| Project-based service company | “The project is delivered before the invoice is issued or chased.” | Turn project completion into an invoice-preparation and follow-up checklist. |
| Construction / field service | “Receipts, supplier invoices and job costs live in too many places.” | Gather documents into a review queue before they become an end-of-month scramble. |
| E-commerce | “Revenue looks healthy, but margins and returns appear too late.” | Create a management summary that flags what needs investigation; do not automate accounting judgment. |

### Why choose it

**Pros**

- Cash, unpaid invoices and hidden costs are owner-level pain.
- Less crowded than generic marketing AI content.
- Strong concrete stakes for Teardowns: a late invoice, missing receipt or unclear cash view.
- Existing finance software can provide a cleaner system foundation than ad-hoc marketing tools.

**Cons**

- Highest credibility and regulatory burden of the three directions.
- Sensitive data makes screenshots, demonstrations and public examples harder.
- A wrong classification or payment action can cause real harm.
- M2 currently should speak only about **finance administration and review workflows**, never accounting, tax or financial advice.

**Strong content angles**

- “Your accountant is not your weekly cash-flow system.”
- “The first finance workflow is not ‘AI does the books’. It is ‘no completed project waits three weeks to be invoiced’.”
- “AI can prepare the invoice-chasing queue. A human still decides how to treat the client relationship.”

## Decision comparison — M2 Lab's current situation

Scores are M2's strategic assessment, **not market statistics**. 5 = more favourable.

| Criterion | Marketing | Sales ops | Finance admin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Easy to make demonstrable Instagram content | 5 | 4 | 3 |
| Owner instantly connects pain to business value | 3 | 5 | 5 |
| Existing M2 proof / ability to build publicly | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| Repeatable workflows for SME | 4 | 5 | 4 |
| Path to a future service offer | 3 | 5 | 4 |
| Risk of harmful / overclaimed advice | 2 | 3 | 5 |
| Risk of generic, crowded content | 5 | 3 | 2 |

### Reading the table

- Choose **Marketing** if the immediate constraint is making high-quality content every week and building public proof through your own workflows.
- Choose **Sales operations** if the immediate constraint is a sharply commercial positioning that can later lead to implementation work.
- Choose **Finance administration** only if you are willing to make the safety boundary part of the positioning and build domain credibility before selling anything.

## Proposed meeting decision

Answer only these four questions:

1. Which pain can Misha and Max explain today without pretending to be specialists?
2. Which process can you build and test on yourselves in the next two weeks?
3. Which result can you show without an unproven revenue claim?
4. Which direction would still be worth making as post number 30?

**Recommended answer today:** Sales operations, specifically lead intake -> next action -> follow-up -> proposal preparation for owner-led service SMEs.

**Boundary:** marketing may appear only when it feeds a lead or sales conversation. Finance may appear only as invoice / receivables / cash-visibility administration with human review.
