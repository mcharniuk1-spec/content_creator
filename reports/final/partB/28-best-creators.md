# 28. Best Creators

**Denominators, and what "best" can mean here.** 132 creators have a `creator_stats` row at snapshot 3;
102 contribute reels to the analysis-ready set. "Best" has to be defined before it is measured, because
the three obvious definitions rank almost disjoint sets of accounts. **Biggest** (median play) is a
statement about audience, not craft. **Steadiest** (CV < 1, n ≥ 8 → `CONSISTENT`) is a statement about
repeatability. **Most forwarded and kept** (median share and save rate) is the only one that measures what
a reel does to the person who watched it — and it is the definition M2 should use, because reach in this
corpus is not a video property (chapter 26).

**`manthanjethwani` (+0.337) is the single most interesting account in the corpus for M2.** He is rising,
he contributes multiple reels to the top of the pool, and his work is the clearest example of demo-first,
screen-evidenced craft on a rising trajectory. He also owns the corpus's highest share rate:
[Dc_tsSeAjBy](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dc_tsSeAjBy/) at **61.3 shares per 1 000** on 2 350 035
plays and 66× his accumulated median.

**Transcript reading.** What that account does is name the exact object and then show it. Dc_tsSeAjBy's
proof beat is **"Look at the other use cases. This is going to Excel, the whole Excel is controlling. And
you can automate whatever you do in Excel."** (29.0–48.0 s), and its close is a genuine question rather
than a gate — **"And this is just the beginning, what do you think? How many people's jobs will be
perfect because of this?"** (85.0–90.0 s), `cta_type = question_to_audience`, so its share rate carries no
gate inflation. His other high reel,
[Dbi-yxNgrhG](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dbi-yxNgrhG/) (240 255 plays, 58.4 shares and 58.9 saves per
1 000), does the same with a universal account and a bounded count: **"If you don't have these four
settings, you can access any private documents or bank statements on your Gmail."** (0.6–7.6 s). A system
everyone already uses, a countable promise, instructions too fast to follow live — so the reel is
forwarded and saved rather than watched.

**Visual reading.** Both reels run `A_ROLL_MEDIUM > SCREEN_RECORDING > …` — face for the claim,
full-frame screen for the evidence, which is chapter 22's pattern. fa-v1 records Dbi-yxNgrhG's proof
visuals as a real Gmail inbox with the Gemini sidebar, the settings dialog and the phone settings page:
three screens, all of them the viewer's own software, none of them a product demo.

## Strategic implication

**Study two accounts properly and copy neither's topic.** `damini.knows` and `divyannshisharma` are the
only creators who are both predictable and above median on forwarding and saving — they are the model for
*how to be a reliable account*. `manthanjethwani` is the model for *what a forwardable reel looks like*:
a system the audience already owns, a bounded count, screen evidence, an ungated close. On selection,
carry `reliability`, CV and baseline `n` onto every reference card, prefer creators with more than one
entry in the pool, and treat any multiplier over ~100× as a fact about a small median. On measurement,
expect M2's own numbers to look like the CONSISTENT rows — small lifts, decent rates — and do not read
that as failure.

**Confidence: RELIABLE** for the roster statistics and reliability labels (n=132, complete, direct
measurement). **PROBABLE** for "who is worth studying", which rests on two accounts and on share/save
comparisons that do not hold `cta_type` constant. **INSUFFICIENT** for the momentum ranking as a
predictor: three snapshots, a trend line rather than a derivative, and an empty `followers` table mean
nothing here can be said about growth.
