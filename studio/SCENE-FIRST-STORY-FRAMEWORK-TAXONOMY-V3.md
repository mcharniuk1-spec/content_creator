# Scene-first Story Framework Taxonomy v3

## Purpose

V3 makes a short video analyzable, writable, generatable, and editable as a sequence of bounded scene-units. A scene-unit is the smallest interval that should be reviewed or generated as one coherent visual/audio operation. It begins or ends when the viewer's visual state changes meaningfully—not merely because a fixed timer elapsed.

The unit is deliberately compatible with both observed footage and clean-room generation:

`transcript span → scene-unit → sampled frames → one scene collage → generation/edit/voice/subtitle instructions`

## Scene boundary rules

Start a new scene-unit when any of these changes attention or meaning:

1. hook or opening state;
2. camera angle, shot scale, lens perspective, or spatial axis;
3. A-roll speaker view, B-roll cutaway, object macro, screen/device insert, or return to speaker;
4. generated scene or creator-shot plate;
5. split-screen or picture-in-picture layout;
6. motion graphic, diagram, UI zoom, caption treatment, or effect that becomes the focal information;
7. material action/state change, reveal, proof, objection, consequence, payoff, or CTA;
8. voice route or audio role change that carries the scene boundary.

Do not split only because a subtitle cue changed, a hand moved slightly, or a held shot contains ordinary motion. Those belong to the same scene-unit unless the viewer's information state changes.

## Start/end and held-scene sampling

Every scene-unit records `start_ms`, `end_ms`, `start_frame`, and `end_frame`. Frame numbers are source-video frame indices when known; timestamps remain authoritative when frame rate is unknown.

Sampling policy:

- **2 frames** — ordinary short scene: start and end only.
- **4 frames** — scene has a meaningful middle action, camera move, caption/effect, or proof progression: start, early-middle, late-middle, end.
- **6 frames** — long hold, multi-step action, sustained effect, split-screen evolution, screen interaction, or continuity risk: start, 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, end.

The sampled frames are never six unrelated thumbnails. They are one ordered visual record. Each scene-unit produces exactly one collage image containing its samples in reading order, with frame IDs/timestamps outside the image or in a sidecar—not baked into the source pixels.

## Scene types

`hook`, `a_roll_direct`, `a_roll_picture_in_picture`, `b_roll_object`, `b_roll_process`, `screen_capture`, `generated_scene`, `creator_shot`, `split_screen`, `motion_graphic`, `proof_reveal`, `transition_bridge`, `cta_or_handback`, `silence_or_beat`.

`creator_shot` means an owner-approved creator recording, not an imitation of a reference creator. `generated_scene` means a separately generated fictional or owner-approved asset. `screen_capture` is a deterministic recording/plate with no private or unapproved authenticated content.

## Transcript and voice binding

Each scene-unit binds to zero or more transcript segment IDs and records a semantic spoken summary. Keep raw transcript text in the run-local evidence layer; use a short permitted excerpt only when rights allow it. Mark whether speech is:

- `direct_creator` — recorded by the on-camera speaker;
- `owner_recorded_voice_over` — owner records narration over B-roll;
- `approved_neutral_tts` — neutral TTS after exact approval;
- `diegetic_scene_audio` — speech belongs inside the generated/recorded scene;
- `no_speech` — music, ambience, SFX, or silence only;
- `unavailable` — evidence or consent is missing.

Do not infer voice identity from a transcript. Voice cloning/likeness is a separate consent and provider gate.

## Scene production routes

Each scene chooses one primary `production_route` and optional layers:

| Mode | Use | Required handoff |
|---|---|---|
| `creator_recorded` | owner/approved speaker A-roll | take brief, framing, spoken line, consent/rights state |
| `fictional_generated_scene` | generated plate/scene animated or generated by editor | still/video prompt, motion prompt, continuity lock, negative prompt |
| `creator_replica` | only an explicitly consented, disclosed replica of a real creator | identity, consent, disclosure, provider, retention/deletion, owner gate; blocked by default |
| `screen_recorded` | deterministic product/process capture | safe demo script, cursor choreography, data/privacy check |
| `split_screen_composite` | two or more synchronized plates | panel map, sync anchor, crop/safe-zone rules |
| `motion_graphic` | diagram, type, UI zoom, data reveal | editable layers, timing, accessibility, source/evidence links |
| `existing_footage_cut` | owner-approved footage | media pointer/hash, rights, trim, continuity and EDL operation |
| `audio_only_over_broll` | voice-over narration | voice stem, transcript timing, B-roll coverage, mix plan |

Never combine a source frame, generated plate, and creator likeness into one unreviewed identity. Each asset layer retains its own provenance and rights state.

## Subtitles, overlays, and audio

Subtitle planning is scene-level and transcript-bound: record caption onset/offset, density, language, safe-zone, emphasis words, and whether captions are burned in or remain editable. Default is `editable_overlay`; generated images do not contain final typography, metrics, claims, or branded UI.

Audio is a separate stem plan: `speech`, `music`, `ambience`, `sfx`, and `silence`. Lock narration timing before motion generation. Record ducking, emphasis, and silence-drop cues. Use only owner-recorded or separately approved licensed/original audio.

## Story and hook placement

The first scene must declare the hook mechanism and the answerable viewer gap: anomaly, contradiction, consequence, object demonstration, result-first, countdown, question, or story-in-progress. The final scene must close or honestly bound that same gap and hand back to A-roll/CTA. B-roll exists to perform a declared information job, not to decorate a transcript.

## Scene collage contract

Required collage properties:

- one collage per scene-unit;
- 2, 4, or 6 ordered samples according to sampling policy;
- visible sequence labels in sidecar metadata and stable frame IDs;
- same crop/aspect and readable subject/object continuity;
- source-frame hashes/pointers separate from generated assets;
- `public_display_allowed` and `rights_state` carried through;
- no raw private media or unapproved creator likeness in the collage.

Collages are review artifacts. They do not replace the original frame files, transcript, timecodes, or an executable edit decision list.

## Quality gates

1. transcript spans cover or explicitly gap the scene;
2. start/end timestamps and frame indices are valid and contiguous;
3. sample count is 2, 4, or 6 and matches the reason;
4. one collage exists and hashes match its manifest;
5. production mode has the required prompt/plate/recording/edit handoff;
6. voice route, subtitle plan, audio stems, and safe zones are explicit;
7. continuity anchor and cut reason are present;
8. source/generation/creator rights and likeness states are separate;
9. maker/reviewer separation is recorded;
10. generated output is labeled previsualization until owner approves the next gate.

## Hierarchy and migration

Use the hierarchy `video → scene-unit → shot/segment → sampled screenshots or generated assets → edit timeline`. The legacy v2 `frames` remain a deterministic six-beat storyboard scaffold. They are not source-evidence screenshots and do not replace scene-unit sampling. New analysis records should use `scene_units`; legacy `video-analysis.schema.json` `scenes`/`shots` may be migrated by copying interval and transcript links, then adding frame bounds, sample roles, collage receipt, production route, voice/consent, and edit-layer fields.

The taxonomy supports scripts and production plans; it does not claim a universal optimal cut rate, retention, virality, or platform performance.
