## Human and scene grammar

- Cast fictional, non-identifiable adults, usually 25–55, by story role rather than stereotype.
- Preserve ordinary facial variation, natural skin texture, restrained expression, and plausible hands. No beauty retouching or reaction-face exaggeration.
- Use matte professional-casual wardrobe without logos. Lock hair, wardrobe, location, time, props, and screen direction across six shots.
- Prefer lived-in home offices, shared desks, modest studios, meeting rooms, and workshops. Avoid luxury, neon-tech, generic influencer sets, and branded interfaces.
- Keep one persistent target, obstacle, trigger, and proof prop visually traceable.

## Camera, light, and edit language

- Master format is 9:16. Medium-close/medium shots carry action; close/macro isolates proof; wide establishes spatial logic.
- Approximate 24–28 mm equivalent for environmental shots and 35–50 mm for close proof shots. Keep perspective believable.
- Use soft window light plus a motivated practical, natural highlight roll-off, modest sharpening, slight shadow noise, and subtle handheld imperfection.
- Avoid cinematic LUTs, anamorphic effects, impossible reflections, aggressive blur, and excessive camera movement.
- Give each beat one visible verb. Cut when information changes.
- Default arc: interrupt/recognition → orientation → obstacle/consequence → attempt → proof/qualification → resolved target and A-roll return.

## Emotion, text, and motion

Use restrained state change: recognition or surprise → concern → agency → confidence-with-caution → relief. Bind technical ideas to physical operations: sort, trace, compare, reveal, test, or inspect. Generated surfaces remain blank/abstract; captions, metrics, UI, claims, and citations are editable overlays. Do not collide dense caption reading with a complex reveal. Motion prompts name subject action, camera action, duration, start/end state, continuity locks, and failure states.

## Prohibited outputs and review

No creator/celebrity likeness, source-composition copying, logos, watermarks, testimonials, fake metrics, brand UI, identity drift, wardrobe changes, warped anatomy, or virality claims. Confirm six readable states, stable continuity, plausible anatomy, full imagery, overlay room, limitation language, and A-roll return. Mechanical validation never replaces visual review.
