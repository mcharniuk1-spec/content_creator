# Studio Screenwriting Guide for Hooks, Intros, and B-roll

Status: `EVIDENCE-BOUNDED / CLEAN-ROOM / PROVIDER-DISABLED`

## The central rule

A short insert is not a stack of attractive shots. It is a compressed event:

> Because **a concrete obstacle changes the situation**, an identifiable **actor** performs an observable **action**, changing a visible **object or state**, so a bounded **consequence** follows—and the viewer can inspect the proof and limitation.

The Studio unit is therefore `state → question → action → changed state`, not “cut every second.” Event-perception research supports organizing experience around meaningful changes in goals, objects, locations, constraints, or predictions; it does not establish a universal social-video cut rate. [LIT-EVENT-01]

## 1. Build the story before the hook

Write these twelve lines before choosing shots:

1. Audience forcing moment: what just happened in their world?
2. Protagonist/POV: who wants something now?
3. Objective: what observable state must change?
4. Obstacle: what concretely prevents it?
5. Stakes: what becomes harder, riskier, slower, or less trustworthy?
6. Scene: where can that conflict be seen?
7. Trigger prop: what object makes the change visible?
8. Hero verb: what can the actor physically do on screen?
9. Proof prop: what can be inspected rather than asserted?
10. Limitation: where does the evidence or mechanism stop?
11. Payoff: which opening question is resolved?
12. Handback: what sentence returns the visual argument to the speaker?

Weak version: “AI improves operations.” Stronger visual premise: “A coordinator sees an urgent request buried in identical tickets, marks urgency and owner, and watches it enter the correct lane; the rule routes work but does not infer human intent.”

## 2. A six-state micro-screenplay

The default v2 insert is six seconds because it gives each required event one readable state. It can sit before, between, or under longer talking-head material.

| Time | Story job | Visual job | Writing test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1s | Hook/inciting change | Show an anomaly, consequence, incomplete action, or contradiction already in progress. | Can the viewer form a precise question without narration? |
| 1–2s | Orientation | Locate actor, object, target, and scene geography. | Is the objective concrete and bounded? |
| 2–3s | Obstacle/stakes | Put resistance between actor and target; show consequence. | What becomes worse if nothing changes? |
| 3–4s | Decision/action | Give the hero one visible verb. | Does the action causally address the obstacle? |
| 4–5s | Proof/limitation | Isolate a receipt, timestamp, comparison, process trace, worked example, or boundary. | What does it support, and what does it not support? |
| 5–6s | Payoff/handback | Resolve or honestly reframe the hook; return to A-roll. | Does the ending answer the opening rather than replace it with a CTA? |

For a 10–12 second insert, hold or expand the action, proof, limitation, and payoff as separate events. For a full >15-second video, chain multiple complete events; do not stretch one event with filler.

## 3. Hook frameworks

The v2 library uses ten families:

- `in_medias_res`: enter after the state changed. “The approval control is already active—but the review card is blank.”
- `result_rewind`: show the clean result, then reveal the decisive missing step.
- `diagnostic_mystery`: expose a symptom and test one clue at a time.
- `failed_attempt_correction`: let the obvious action fail, then turn failure into information.
- `object_metaphor`: make an abstract mechanism physical, then test the metaphor with a real state.
- `countdown_ladder`: promise a finite path whose last item is a proof/constraint check.
- `before_after_transformation`: contrast states, show the converting action, and retain the limitation.
- `proof_reveal`: open on a claim with a deliberately empty proof slot.
- `contradiction_resolution`: show two facts that seem incompatible, then supply the missing distinction.
- `process_handback`: complete an input-to-output action under voice-over and return with the checked result.

YouTube’s own 2025 creator discussion emphasizes immediate hooks and compact mini-stories/moments; treat this as platform/creator guidance, not causal performance evidence. [PLAT-YT-01] TikTok’s June 2025 advertising guidance recommends an early proposition, vertical safe-zone-aware composition, captions, a hook/body/CTA structure, and continued testing; it explicitly presents these as starting points rather than universal results. [PLAT-TT-01]

### Hook quality test

A hook passes only when it is:

- concrete: it contains a noun the viewer can see;
- consequential: the viewer understands why the state matters;
- answerable: the promised gap can close inside the planned duration;
- matched: the payoff answers the same question;
- honest: it does not imply proof, certainty, urgency, or results that the content lacks.

Information-gap theory is useful here: curiosity needs a meaningful missing piece, not random novelty. It does not prescribe one universal gap size. [LIT-CURIOSITY-01]

## 4. How to touch the audience without manipulating them

Use an ethical emotional arc:

`recognition → concern → agency → cautious relief`

- Recognition: name a forcing moment the audience has plausibly experienced.
- Concern: show the consequence in the object/state, not through vague fear.
- Agency: let the hero make a bounded decision viewers can understand.
- Relief: show the changed state and remaining boundary.

Narrative-transportation research motivates coherent POV, goals, actions, and consequences, but does not prove that any short video will persuade, convert, or perform. [LIT-TRANS-01]

Avoid fearbait, shame, fake deadlines, manufactured controversy, unsupported social proof, or holding back the promised answer solely to force a click.

## 5. Explain technical detail while keeping a story alive

Use the technical-detail ladder:

1. Concrete problem object: “three notes disagree.”
2. Mechanism verb: “sort by decision owner.”
3. Visible state change: “one accountable route remains.”
4. Proof object: “a dated decision receipt.”
5. Boundary: “routing does not prove business success.”

Reveal only one new cognitive job per state: locate, read, compare, infer, or verify. Dense captions and a complex visual reveal should not begin on the same cut; eye-tracking evidence shows subtitles affect gaze during shot changes under the studied conditions. [LIT-SUB-01]

Translate abstract nouns into production verbs:

| Abstract word | Visible verb/object |
|---|---|
| governance | a gate holds the action until a review token arrives |
| provenance | a line connects a claim card to a dated source tile |
| freshness | a timestamp is revealed before the number |
| orchestration | different work cards route through one controlled junction |
| memory | a request retrieves one labeled prior record |
| safety | the normal path continues while an exception moves to a human lane |

## 6. B-roll is an argument, not decoration

Adobe describes B-roll as supplemental footage that establishes context, bridges edits, adds meaning, and supports the A-roll/plot; its B-Script research summary reports expert editors often placed B-roll around narratively relevant keywords. These are production/research-context findings, not universal retention laws. [PROD-ADOBE-01, PROD-ADOBE-02]

Every cutaway gets exactly one declared function:

- establish context;
- concretize an abstraction;
- demonstrate a process;
- prove a state;
- contrast alternatives;
- reset attention at an event boundary;
- add ethical emotional texture;
- orient a generic interface.

Choose the first concrete noun or verb in narration as a candidate cut point. Then ask whether the image actually changes what the viewer knows. If it does not, keep A-roll.

## 7. Frame, action, and camera logic

Movement must be motivated by information:

- push in: isolate the inciting anomaly;
- pull out: reveal actor/target geography or the resolved whole;
- pan with action: preserve screen direction while an object changes lanes;
- rack focus: transfer attention from action to proof;
- match on action: carry the same hand/object movement across a cut;
- locked frame: let the obstacle or comparison be inspected;
- deliberate discontinuity: signal a true scene/event boundary, not random novelty.

Continuity research indicates that viewer goals affect post-cut gaze and that recognisable anchors across viewpoint changes matter in the studied scenes. Preserve at least one anchor: actor, object, screen position, motion vector, audio bridge, or semantic color. [LIT-CUT-01]

## 8. Captions, text, sound, and safe zones

- Keep final captions, charts, labels, and UI editable; do not bake small final text into generated imagery.
- One frame should not ask the viewer to read a dense caption, inspect a new proof object, and reorient to a new scene simultaneously.
- Use narration as the anchor. Sound accents should mark state changes, not fill every cut.
- A silence drop can protect a proof or limitation beat.
- Plan in 9:16 with conservative insets, then verify current platform UI before production. TikTok’s guidance confirms vertical 9:16 and safe-zone awareness for ads, but the actual safe area varies by placement and UI. [PLAT-TT-01]

## 9. Posting and testing

This library is not an algorithm recipe. Platform discovery is personalized and affected by viewer behavior, topic interest, competition, and seasonality; a creative pattern cannot be credited for a public count without owned experimental evidence. [PLAT-YT-02]

For owned content:

1. Freeze one script and one audience/job.
2. Test one variable: hook state, first sentence, proof object, or handback—not all simultaneously.
3. Keep aspect, duration, caption treatment, publication window, and targeting as comparable as practical.
4. Predefine the decision metric and minimum sample; do not select a winner from noise.
5. Record retention/skip/rewatch and qualitative confusion separately from likes/comments.
6. Preserve losing versions and caveats; a result is local to audience, topic, time, and execution.
7. Refresh platform specifications before production/publishing.

## 10. Clean-room originality

Analyze sources at the level of function: hook class, event boundary, shot scale, proof form, transition reason, and viewer job. Never reproduce transcript wording, named characters, branded UI, distinctive metaphor, shot order, composition, music, face, voice, or CTA. For related concepts, change at least one semantic dimension, one causal topology/obstacle-action relation, and one visual mechanism.
