# M2 Lab — Positioning and Monetization Playbook

## 1. The Decision

M2 Lab is an English-language media and future service business about **practical AI adoption for non-technical small-business leaders**.

M2 Lab will not position itself as a sales, marketing, finance, or industry-specialist agency. It will not promise revenue growth, conversion uplift, or operational transformation before it has real client evidence.

Its territory is narrower and more credible:

> Helping a non-technical leader move from AI confusion to one useful, human-controlled workflow.

## 2. Public Positioning

> **M2 Lab helps non-technical small-business leaders turn AI confusion into clear, useful everyday work — one workflow at a time, without coding or chasing every new tool.**

Supporting line:

> **Understand what matters. Start with one repeated task. Keep a human in control.**

This is the promise to the audience. It is not a claim that M2 Lab will automate an entire company or guarantee a business result.

## 3. Internal Operating Statement

> **We help the person responsible for AI adoption in a small business choose one repeated task, map it clearly, and build the first human-controlled workflow.**

The M2 method:

`repeated task → process map → AI boundary → human review → first test`

Every content piece, offer, and future product must reinforce this method.

## 4. Who M2 Lab Serves

### Core audience

The person responsible for figuring out where AI should actually fit in a small business.

This is one job-to-be-done, not one job title. It may be:

- an owner or CEO in a small company;
- an operations lead;
- a team lead responsible for a recurring process;
- a functional manager asked to “figure out AI” for the team.

They know AI exists and may have tried ChatGPT or Claude. They do not know which task to start with, which tools matter, what can be trusted, or how to introduce AI without creating more chaos.

### Roles in a future client engagement

| Role | Who they are | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Owner or CEO | Clarity, lower chaos, and a team that can use AI responsibly. |
| Champion | Operations or team lead | A clear first process and a realistic implementation path. |
| User | The person doing the repeated task | A workflow that makes work easier without removing their judgment. |

### Audience level

M2 Lab serves people from approximately AI level 0 to 6 out of 10:

- They may know the main AI tools but have no repeatable way of using them at work.
- They are open to practical no-code or low-code workflows.
- They need plain-language explanations and real examples.

M2 Lab does not serve advanced technical builders who want local models, deep orchestration, custom infrastructure, or multi-agent architecture.

## 5. What M2 Lab Is and Is Not

### M2 Lab is

- A practical AI adoption media brand.
- A translator between AI capability and a real repeated work process.
- A builder in public: showing tests, trade-offs, failures, and human-review boundaries.
- A future education, diagnostic, and scoped implementation business.

### M2 Lab is not

- A generic AI news account.
- A tool-list or prompt-hack account.
- A “make money with AI” account.
- A sales, marketing, finance, legal, or HR expert brand.
- A promise to automate every business process.
- An “AI employee” vendor selling autonomous systems without process ownership and human approval.

## 6. Credibility Boundary

Misha and Max can credibly:

- explain AI to non-technical people at a beginner-to-intermediate level;
- break a repeated process into steps, inputs, outputs, decisions, and risks;
- assess where AI can assist and where a human must remain responsible;
- research tools and market signals;
- build and test a scoped workflow;
- document what worked, what failed, and what should not be automated.

M2 Lab must not claim that it can:

- guarantee revenue, conversion, retention, cost savings, or productivity gains;
- advise on a business function where it lacks real expertise or cases;
- build a reliable solution for every reviewed tool or workflow;
- replace professional legal, tax, medical, HR, credit, or security judgment;
- deploy AI that makes external decisions or takes actions without human approval.

Internal builds are **experiments**, not client case studies. Use “we tested,” “we built,” and “this broke.” Use “client result” only after a real client pilot with an agreed baseline and metric.

## 7. Language and Geography

### Language

- Public content: English only.
- Website and offers: English only.
- Live workshops and cohorts: English only.

### Geography

- Do not name a country in the public positioning during the first 60 days.
- The content audience is non-technical, English-working small-business leaders.
- The initial commercial focus is English-working SMEs in Europe.
- Netherlands and Ireland are research and outreach hypotheses, not brand identities.

Do not claim “for Dutch SMEs” or “for European businesses” until there are real local conversations, pilots, or a repeated country-specific problem.

## 8. Content System

M2 Lab has three content pillars. Education is not a fourth pillar; it must be embedded in all three.

| Pillar | Purpose | Core audience question | Required proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M2 Radar** | Translate a tool, model, or market signal into a work decision. | “Should I test this in my team?” | A concrete task, decision, and limitation. |
| **M2 Builds** | Show a workflow M2 actually built and tested. | “What does a first working version look like?” | Process, result, failure, and human review. |
| **M2 Teardowns** | Break down a repeated process before automation. | “Is this process suitable for AI?” | Inputs, friction, AI boundary, owner, and risk. |

### Mandatory editorial filter

Do not publish a Reels unless it makes clear:

1. Which repeated work process is being discussed.
2. Where the real friction is.
3. What AI should do and what remains human-owned.
4. What the viewer should do next.

### Do not publish

- Model news without a practical work decision.
- Generic “top AI tools” lists.
- Deep technical tutorials aimed at developers.
- AI-agent demos without a real process owner.
- Revenue, conversion, or time-saving claims without evidence.
- Content about automation for its own sake.

### Proof loop

M2 must continuously create its own evidence:

`process teardown → small internal test → what broke → revised workflow → content → next process`

Do not wait for clients before building proof. Do not run out of internal evidence and become a news-reposting account.

## 9. Monetization Paths

M2 Lab can combine several revenue models over time. They should all reinforce the same position: practical AI adoption for non-technical business leaders.

### Paid community for business owners and operators

**Possible format:** a membership such as **AI Adoption Lab** for people responsible for introducing AI into a real business process.

**Value:** members receive recurring help to choose the next process, review workflows, learn from real M2 builds and failures, use practical templates and SOPs, and get peer accountability from other operators facing the same implementation problem.

**What makes it worth paying for:** not access to a chat, tool news, or generic AI lessons. The recurring value is a practical implementation environment: workflow clinics, process reviews, tested playbooks, and a peer group with the same job-to-be-done.

**Reality:** this is a later model. A membership is viable only when M2 can reliably create recurring value and there is a concentrated group with the same repeated problem. The first test should be a small, time-boxed implementation cohort, not an endless subscription.

### Paid consulting and workshops

**Possible format:** team workshops, AI adoption sessions, or workflow diagnostics for a business owner or team lead.

**Value:** the client gains clarity on what AI is useful for in their context, which repeated process to examine first, and what should remain human-owned.

**What M2 can sell honestly:** practical AI education, process mapping, task selection, workflow review, and an implementation brief for one scoped opportunity.

**Boundary:** do not sell an “AI transformation strategy,” business-growth consulting, or advice in a functional domain where M2 has no evidence or expertise.

### Custom AI workflow solutions

**Possible format:** a fixed-scope build for one repeated, low-risk workflow.

**Value:** the client receives a working first version of an AI-assisted process, including a human approval step, simple SOP, ownership, and quality checks.

**Best-fit use cases:** drafting, summarising, extracting, classifying, preparing, searching internal materials, and other repeatable knowledge-work tasks with clear inputs and outputs.

**Boundary:** M2 does not sell “automate our whole company.” Each build requires a named process owner, clear data access, defined inputs and outputs, a low cost of error, and human review. Do not take legal, tax, medical, hiring, credit, or autonomous external-action workflows.

### Cohorts and practical education products

**Possible format:** a short paid cohort, mini-course, workshop series, playbook, template pack, or process-mapping kit.

**Value:** a lower-cost way for non-technical leaders to understand AI, select a suitable process, and create a first controlled workflow without buying custom consulting.

**Reality:** build these only after the same questions and process patterns repeat in the audience or client work. Do not create a course because it feels scalable; create it because a repeated problem has already been proven.

### Brand partnerships, sponsorships, and affiliate deals

**Possible format:** sponsored M2 Radar episodes, paid educational partnerships, affiliate links, or tool partnerships.

**Value to a brand:** credible access to a focused audience of non-technical business leaders exploring practical AI adoption.

**Value to the audience:** an honest explanation of where a tool fits, where it does not fit, and what to check before using it.

**Boundary:** only partner with tools M2 has genuinely tested or can evaluate responsibly. Never let a sponsor turn M2 Radar into tool promotion. The audience must still receive the M2 verdict, limitations, and human-review boundary.

### Platform revenue from Instagram and YouTube

**Possible format:** Instagram monetisation features where available, YouTube Partner Program revenue, paid brand integrations, and long-form YouTube educational content that drives viewers into owned offers.

**Value:** content revenue can eventually offset production costs and create a second revenue stream.

**Reality:** platform revenue should not be the business model at the beginning. For a focused B2B audience, the higher-value path is usually trust that converts into a community, consulting, workshops, or scoped solutions. YouTube is valuable because it can build deeper trust, create searchable proof, and feed the same offers—not because ad revenue will arrive quickly.

### Future software product

**Possible format:** a lightweight product, workflow template, or AI adoption tool built around one repeated paid problem.

**Value:** a scalable way to solve a common task that M2 has already observed and validated through content, cohorts, diagnostics, or client work.

**Boundary:** do not build software from an interesting idea alone. Build it only after the same pain and required workflow repeat across multiple paying users or clients.

## Final Principle

M2 Lab does not sell AI hype or a tool catalogue.

It helps a non-technical business leader make one good decision about one real process, build a controlled first version, and learn enough to move forward responsibly.
