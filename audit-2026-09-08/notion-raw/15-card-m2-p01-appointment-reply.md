## The idea in one minute
**For:** Reception or operations lead designing appointment reminders.
**Problem:** An uncertain reply falls between the happy-path categories and may be treated as a confirmed change.
**What this video gives the viewer:** Keep uncertainty visible, route the reply to a named role, and define a next step for each branch.
**Why this approach:** The ordinary phrase "Maybe next week" is the whole problem. The branching drawing answers it without requiring software or an advanced AI explanation.
Teardowns · Regular publication · Original synthesis of several references · about 60 seconds, 131 spoken words.
## Spoken script
What happens when the customer replies, 'Maybe next week'? An appointment demo might show a booking, a reminder, and a happy confirmation. Put that uncertain reply into the middle. Now draw three branches: confirmed, wants a change, and unclear. Add a fourth for no response. AI could suggest a category and draft the next message. In your first test, keep the original reply visible and check the suggestion. For 'maybe next week,' show the staff member a question to resolve. Don't quietly turn it into a confirmed date. Give each branch an owner and an agreed next step. You can sketch this with paper before connecting any software. Take one reminder your business sends. Write the most awkward reply underneath it. Does your process have somewhere for that reply to go?
