# 33. Recommendations for Our Positioning

**Denominators, one last time.** Everything below rests on 268 analysed reels (8.3 % of 3 211 ingested),
266 semantic reads, 264 beat parses, 295 frame reads at 9 fixed samples each, and 245 rows with a save
rate. The analysed corpus is the top of `score.py`'s ranking, so it describes what the *already-successful*
part of this niche sells [I-01]. Nothing here is causal.

## 1. What viewers in this niche are actually looking for

Across 266 semantic reads the demand is not for understanding. It is for **a thing you can have** — a
repo, a file, a workflow, a prompt, a map — that removes a named piece of manual work, named tool by named
tool, with the screen showing it running. Nine of the thirteen RELIABLE correlations in the whole
association run are save-rate correlations, and a save is a bet that the thing will be used.

| the demand | measurement | what it means for M2 |
|---|---|---|
| **an artefact to take away** | ready-made-artefact desire n=27: save **0.0346** vs 0.0270 (p=0.052); `resource_handoff` save **0.0429** (n=38, 28 creators), the highest of any solution type | publish a checklist, process map or prompt with every reel |
| **a named repeated act, not a condition** | `manual_repetition` view_lift **6.94** / save 0.0390 (n=15); `time_waste` 3.54 / 0.0365 (n=20); `chaos_no_process` **0.87** (n=17); `slow_response_to_leads` **0.47** (n=5) | "you retype the same numbers out of a PDF every Monday", never "your process is chaotic" |
| **named destinations** | `lex_platforms_n` → save_rate **+0.331** (cn +0.173), RELIABLE; `lex_entities_distinct` → view_lift +0.161 (cn +0.198) | name Claude, then the Google Sheet, then the inbox — three destinations, not one repeated |
| **time back, in minutes** | time-saving desire n=33: view_lift 3.52 vs 1.88, save 0.0330 vs 0.0275 (p=0.025) | quantify in minutes in the hook |
| **screen evidence at ~22 s** | median time to first proof beat **22.0 s**; 41 % of 2 645 frames are proof visuals; `screen_demo` is the largest proof type (n=84) | the run on screen, at the twenty-second mark |
| **not certainty on its own** | "does it actually work" desire n=19: save **0.0140 vs 0.0282** (p=0.017, negative) | a decision must arrive attached to an object |
| **not news** | model-news topic save **0.0097**, a third of the corpus median; `announcement_news` view_lift 1.29 (n=30) | already excluded by POSITIONING §8; the data agrees |
| **not status as an entry ticket** | `identity_call` hook: median view_lift **0.41** (n=9), the worst in the corpus | status may be a payoff clause, never the opening |
| **not urgency** | urgency → view_lift −0.164; fear → share_rate −0.157; both negative on every metric | name a real risk once, then drop the register |

The uncomfortable half of that table: **M2's declared territory is the least-served and lowest-performing
labelled cell in the measured niche.** `AI в конкретном бизнес-процессе` is n=8 across 8 creators at
view_lift **0.65**, robust_z 0.85 and share rate 0.0040 — a quarter of the corpus median. The topic M2 is
positioned to refuse, agent building, is n=27 at **6.63×** [I-25]. Eight reels cannot prove the subject
fails, and the selection bias runs the other way; what they do establish is that nobody is producing it at
volume. **The audience narrowing itself is free**: developer-audience reels run at median view_lift 1.87
against 1.89 for business-audience reels — no measurable difference [I-27].

## 3. The mandatory four-part filter, now measurable

Do not publish a reel unless it makes clear: **(1) which repeated work process is being discussed,
(2) where the real friction is, (3) what AI should do and what remains human-owned, (4) what the viewer
should do next.** Three of the four are now checkable against the data rather than against taste.

Part 1 is the pain-as-act rule (6.94× against 0.87×). Part 2 costs 8–10 seconds inside the first quarter,
and M2 pays for them out of the mechanism block — the strong quartile already spends **34.1 %** of speech
on explanation against 41.9 % for the weak one, reallocating into solution (**12.25 s vs 8.4 s,
p=0.0065**) and payoff. Part 3 has exactly one precedent in 266 reels, and it is worth quoting because it
is the entire M2 thesis said by a competitor in passing:
[DbVKVz0y8xo](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DbVKVz0y8xo/) — **"Because the AI knows how to cut a video
but it doesn't know the exact moment that needs to be a clip. It has no context and no content
intelligence."** (13.5–31.4 s). Part 4 is one ask, on the face, in the last 10 % of the script.

## 4. No comment-bait — and the data says the ban is free

**59 % of the analysed corpus (158 of 266) runs a comment-keyword gate.** It multiplies comment rate
**×23**, save **×2.8** and share **×1.8**, and moves reach by nothing at all: view_lift 2.08 against 1.72
(p=0.58), robust_z **1.2475 against 1.2481** (p=0.63) [I-12]. RULES §1.2 already bans it on positioning
grounds; the measurement adds that **no distribution is forfeited**. Two consequences follow. M2's own
published share and save rates will be structurally lower than gated references' and must never be
compared with them. And selection must hold `cta_type` constant, because `cards.py` currently ranks on
`resh_1k` and `save_1k` — the two rates the gate inflates.

The move that replaces the gate is already visible in the corpus. The purest gated reel,
[DVtgbY8Av6A](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DVtgbY8Av6A/), buys **57.3 saves per 1 000** from 84 words by
naming fifteen skills and withholding the files. **Invert it:** name the artefact and publish it in the
caption. The save comes from there being something to collect, not from having to ask [I-29].

## 5. The 50–70 second one-take template — keep it, and stop citing it as evidence

Keep the template: one location and light, one banner, one caption system, the screen as a full-frame
insert, one closing line, **50–70 seconds**, and a change of speaker as the only permitted cut. It suits
one filming day and five publications a week, and **nothing in the data argues against it**. But it is a
production convenience, not a measured reach lever, and two numbers currently used to justify it do not
reproduce.

| PRODUCTION.md claim | source of the original | what reproduces on 3 211 creator-normalised reels |
|---|---|---|
| "the winners' median is **55 seconds** against **47** for the rest" | a 100-reel top slice of an earlier 2 352-reel export | **46.8 s** for the 375 HIGH outliers against **47.8 s** for the other 2 836 — a difference of one second, in the opposite direction |
| "under twenty seconds is **5 %** of winners and **13 %** of the rest" | same slice | **14 %** of HIGH outliers against **12 %** of the rest — the direction is **reversed** |

Duration correlates with `robust_z` at **−0.069** across 3 211 reels (p=9.5e-05, significant only because
n is large) and with `view_lift` at −0.0004; the band medians are flat (under 20 s **0.25**, 20–45 s
**0.00**, 45–70 s **−0.01**, 70–90 s **−0.19**, over 90 s **+0.05**) [I-02]. A third published number
needs the same correction: **RULES §7's "a share separates a winner 3.6× more reliably, a save 2.8×"
becomes ×2.62 and ×2.09** on the 2 899 reels clearing the 100-play guard [I-28]. Neither original is wrong
about direction; both are wrong about magnitude, and all three should be edited before anyone quotes them
again.

On the one-take rule specifically: cut frequency shows no relation to anything (rho +0.056 on robust_z,
p=0.36) and the metric is a scene-score threshold count that cannot be quoted as an edit count at all.
Keep the rule because it is cheap. **Never publish a claim about M2's cut rate.**

## 6. The ten decisions, in order

1. **Own screen in every reel, and in frame one** — the only RELIABLE production instruction (share
   +0.203; first-frame screen, creator-normalised **+0.285**).
2. **Face → screen → face**, CTA on the face (39 reels, 32 creators, share 0.0213 vs 0.0140).
3. **Never split screen** (22.3 % of the niche's frames, robust_z 1.03 vs 1.43).
4. **Hook: one complete promise, 5–10 s, 20–30 words, a statement, opening on the artefact** — never on
   the series, the audience or the speaker (setup-first 0.67 vs 1.36, p=0.002).
5. **Three distinct named destinations per script**, and repeat the names rather than varying them.
6. **Friction as a named act with minutes, 8–10 s, first quarter.**
7. **Mechanism capped at a third; solution statement ~12 s; payoff names what changes on Monday.**
8. **Proof at ~22 s, on screen, M2's own run, including the failure.**
9. **One ask, ungated, last 10 % of the script, ~5 s and 20 words.**
10. **Judge on shares and saves per 1 000, with `cta_type` and creator `reliability` recorded** — and
    expect small multipliers once M2's own account becomes consistent (CONSISTENT creators: median
    robust_z 0.60 against HIGH_VARIANCE 1.37, with identical script shape).

**Confidence.** Recommendations 1, 5 (as a save lever) and the question ban are **RELIABLE**.
Recommendations 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9 are **PROBABLE** — real, but resting on categorical medians or on
cells of 15 to 42 reels. The "what viewers are looking for" table is **PROBABLE** on its three largest
rows and **INSUFFICIENT** on status, certainty and novelty (n<20 each). The topic verdict on M2's own
territory is **PROBABLE at best and INSUFFICIENT as a prediction**: eight reels across eight creators
cannot establish that a subject fails, and this corpus is 8.3 % of the database, pre-selected for
strength. **No recommendation above is a causal claim, and none of them promises reach.**
