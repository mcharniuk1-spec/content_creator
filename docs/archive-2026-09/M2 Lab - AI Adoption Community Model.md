# M2 Lab — AI Adoption Community Model

## Decision

Do not launch a paid Skool community now.

M2 Lab does not yet have the proof, member density, or recurring value required for a sustainable subscription. Launching a generic “AI community for business owners” would split focus away from the immediate priority: publishing consistent content and proving the M2 method through real builds.

Build a waitlist now. Test a paid implementation cohort later.

## Positioning Context

> **M2 Lab helps non-technical small-business leaders turn AI confusion into clear, useful everyday work — one workflow at a time, without coding or chasing every new tool.**

The audience is the person responsible for deciding where AI should actually fit in a small business: an owner, CEO, operations lead, or team lead.

M2 Lab is not a general AI education brand, an AI news channel, or an “AI employee” agency. The core method is:

`repeated task → process map → AI boundary → human review → first test`

## The Community That Could Work

Not:

> A community about AI tools, news, prompts, and automation.

Instead:

> **AI Adoption Lab**  
> A small working group for non-technical people responsible for introducing AI into a real business process.

The promise is not “learn AI.” The promise is:

> Choose one repeated process, map it, build a first human-controlled AI workflow, and know how to use it responsibly.

## Recommended First Format: 4-Week Implementation Cohort

Do not begin with an ongoing membership. Run a small, time-boxed cohort first.

| Week | Participant outcome |
|---|---|
| 1 | One suitable repeated process is selected. |
| 2 | A process map exists: inputs, steps, output, risks, owner, and review point. |
| 3 | A first AI-assisted version is built and tested with human review. |
| 4 | The participant has a simple SOP, quality checks, and a next-step plan. |

### What M2 provides

- A decision framework for choosing the first process.
- Process-mapping templates.
- Examples of M2 builds, failures, and trade-offs.
- Weekly live workflow clinics.
- Peer accountability and feedback.
- A simple implementation checklist.

### What M2 does not provide

- Unlimited custom automation work.
- A bespoke AI strategy for every company.
- Revenue, conversion, or productivity guarantees.
- High-risk workflows involving legal, tax, hiring, medical, credit, or autonomous external actions.
- “AI employees” operating without a human owner and approval step.

## Launch Conditions

Do not run the first cohort until all conditions are true:

1. M2 has published for 60 days / 24 posts without changing the audience, promise, or three content pillars.
2. At least 10 people have independently expressed the same problem: choosing and implementing a first useful workflow.
3. M2 has documented at least three honest builds, including what failed and where human review remained necessary.
4. Both founders can reliably run a live English session every week and support participants between sessions within a defined scope.
5. The offer is sold as a cohort with a concrete outcome, not as access to a chat group.

Follower count is not a launch condition. Repeated high-intent demand is.

## Path to an Ongoing Membership

An ongoing Skool membership becomes viable only after one or two cohorts show that members want continued support.

The recurring value must be concrete:

- Weekly Workflow Clinic.
- New process reviews.
- A library of tested templates and SOPs.
- New M2 builds and post-mortems.
- Peer feedback from other AI adoption owners.

If people only want “how to use ChatGPT,” the product is a short course, not a community.

## Monetization Path

`Content → waitlist → implementation cohort → workflow diagnostic → one-workflow pilot → enablement/support → membership or templates`

The first paid offer should be a cohort or a workflow diagnostic. Do not sell a broad implementation agency before there is a clear, scoped client problem.

## Operating Rule

Skool is only infrastructure. It is not the product.

The product is a repeatable outcome for a narrowly defined person: helping a non-technical business leader move one real process from AI confusion to a useful, controlled workflow.
