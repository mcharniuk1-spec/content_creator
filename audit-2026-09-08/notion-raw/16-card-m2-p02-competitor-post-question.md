## The idea in one minute
**For:** Small-business marketer studying competitor content.
**Problem:** A large view count tempts the team to copy a hook without understanding the customer question it answered.
**What this video gives the viewer:** Use dated performance to choose what to inspect, then build an original answer to a customer need.
**Why this approach:** The three-note worksheet turns reference watching into a repeatable editorial decision. Closing the reference before drafting helps separate inspiration from imitation.
Radar · Regular publication · Original synthesis of several references · about 60 seconds, 134 spoken words.
## Spoken script
That competitor post got a lot of views. Which customer question made it worth watching? If you help market a small business, use the number to choose something to inspect. Keep the capture date beside it. Compare it with the same account's usual views. Then read what was said and look at what the viewer could actually see. Write three notes: the problem introduced, the useful answer, and the visual that made that answer clear. Now close the reference. Pick a question your own customers ask, and answer it with your own example. A high view count doesn't tell you which detail caused it. Treat your new idea as something to test, not a proven recipe. Before borrowing a hook, finish this sentence: our customer needs to understand blank. Build the post around that.
