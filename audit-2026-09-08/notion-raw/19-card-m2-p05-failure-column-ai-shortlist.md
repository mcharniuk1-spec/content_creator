## The idea in one minute
**For:** Operations lead comparing tools for a repeated reporting task.
**Problem:** Polished drafts distract from omissions and the work required to detect and correct them.
**What this video gives the viewer:** Compare a shared input against explicit failure checks, then assign detection and correction ownership.
**Why this approach:** The missing stock item anchors an original synthesis in a single pain. The final three-column checklist turns that insight into an immediate next action.
Radar · Regular publication · Original synthesis of several references · about 60 seconds, 128 spoken words.
## Spoken script
Your AI tool comparison needs a failure column. Imagine two tools preparing the same weekly stock note. One draft looks sharper. Both leave out an item. Before choosing, write down how your team would spot that missing item. Can you compare the draft with the original list? Now test an invented number, a late draft, and a mistaken send instruction. Decide what should happen in each case. Keep the comparison simple: the same fictional input, the same checks, and a person responsible for reviewing the result. Include the work needed to correct an error. A polished first answer is only one part of the job. Add three columns to your shortlist: failure, how we'll notice, and who fixes it. Which failure would matter most for your team's task?
