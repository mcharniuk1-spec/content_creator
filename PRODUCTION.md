# How an M2 Lab reel is built

Measured on our own set of 2 352 competitor reels, not taken from anyone's advice.
This file answers "how do we shoot it". What we may talk about at all is decided by
`POSITIONING.md`, and that document wins wherever the two seem to disagree.

---

## The template

**Fixed:** one location and light · a banner in one place, one typeface, held for the whole
reel · one caption style · the screen shown as a full-frame insert · **50–70 seconds** ·
one closing line.

**Variable:** who is on camera and how many — one, both, or Misha first and Max after.

**A change of speaker is the only cut we allow.** Everything else is shot in one take.
Ground: of the top hundred reels in the niche, 44 have effectively no cuts and 12 have
exactly zero, and the difference in signal against edited ones sits inside the noise.

**Why 50–70 seconds:** the winners' median is 55 seconds against 47 for the rest. Under
twenty seconds is 5% of winners and 13% of the rest. Longer than ninety is 15% against 8%.

## The two questions before filming

**Why would anyone forward this?** A share separates a winner 3.6× more reliably than
anything else; a save, 2.8×. If neither answer is obvious, the reel is not ready.

**Can a non-technical viewer follow it without pausing?** The audience is level 0–6 out of
ten. Jargon, a terminal, or a tool name in the first three seconds loses them, and the
positioning document rules that content out anyway.

## What the numbers say about topics

**Our territory looks weak and is not.** "AI inside a business process" runs at 22 shares
per thousand against 38 for the window. But 29 of the 62 lead-generation reels come from two
agency accounts advertising their own product, and the ceiling of the same topic is
**2 755 shares** — a reel that hands over a concrete process instead of describing a service.
Hundredfold gap. The topic is open for whoever treats it as content rather than a pitch.

**Comment bait does not work.** Present in 28% of top reels and 26% of the rest.

**In a process reel we never name our own service.** That is what separates the median from
the ceiling above.

## Proof we can show today

`POSITIONING.md` requires every claim to rest on evidence we own, and rules that internal
builds are experiments, not client results. Today that stock is:

- **Process work on our own operation** — what we do repeatedly, where it leaks, what we
  handed to a machine and what we deliberately did not.
- **Tool tests with an outcome** — including the ones where the answer was "not worth it".
- **Niche measurements** — 2 352 reels, a hundred taken apart frame by frame. Useful as
  context, never as a client result.

**The gap to close.** The proof loop in the positioning runs
`process teardown → small internal test → what broke → revised workflow → content`.
We have not yet taken apart a single ordinary business process end to end — everything we
have measured so far is our own tooling, which is not what the audience runs on a Monday.
Until that changes, Teardowns will be the hardest of the three formats to fill honestly.
