# 06. Content formats

Three pillars. Education is not a fourth pillar; it lives inside all three.

## The three pillars

| Pillar | Purpose | Core audience question | Required proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M2 Radar** | Translate a tool, model or market signal into a work decision. | "Is this solution relevant to me?" | A concrete task, a decision, and a limitation. |
| **M2 Builds** | Show a workflow M2 actually built and tested. | "What does a first working version look like?" | Process, result, failure, and human review. |
| **M2 Teardowns** | Break down a repeated process before automation. | "Is this process suitable for AI, and how would it work?" | Inputs, friction, AI boundary, owner, and risk. |

## Structure at 50 to 60 seconds

Same five beats in all three formats. The bands are targets, not a stopwatch.

| Beat | Band | Radar | Builds | Teardowns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-3 s | The one work decision this signal forces. | The thing we ran and how long for. | The process and where its time goes. |
| Context | 3-12 s | The task it touches and who owns it today. | What we were trying to replace, and the baseline. | The current steps, in order. |
| Proof | 12-38 s | Our own test on one task, plus the limitation we hit. | The screen, the run, and the step that broke. | The map: CURRENT, AI LAYER, VERDICT, with the step that stays human. |
| Verdict or conclusion | 38-50 s | KEEP, TEST or KILL with the condition when the piece makes a call; otherwise the one-sentence conclusion of the proof. | Verdict Card on screen, plus the WAS / NOW / SAVED number, when the piece makes a call; otherwise the one-sentence conclusion of the proof. | Verdict Card on screen: which steps to automate, which not, when the piece makes a call; otherwise the one-sentence conclusion of the proof. |
| Next step | 50-60 s | One thing they do in their own work today. | One thing they do, or the workflow file in the channel. | One thing they do before their next process review. |

The Verdict Card is used when the piece delivers a call on a tool or a process. It is
not required in every piece; a piece without a verdict ends its proof with one plain
conclusion. When a verdict is given it is shape-coded and readable with the sound off.

## Mandatory editorial filter

Do not publish a Reel unless it makes clear:

1. Which repeated work process is being discussed.
2. Where the real friction is.
3. What AI should do and what remains human-owned.
4. What the viewer should do next.

Four yes answers, or it goes back. There is no fifth question that saves a piece.

## One piece, three readers

The same Reel is read by three people, and it has to work for all three at once.

| Reader | What the piece has to do for them |
|---|---|
| Owner | Leave them engaged, seeing the problem as their own. |
| Champion | Leave them informed, seeing the solution and the path to it. |
| User | Show the problem and the solution simply, in their own words. |

A Reel shows the problem and the solution, so all three read it.

## Do not publish

- Model news without a practical work decision.
- Generic "top AI tools" lists.
- Deep technical tutorials aimed at developers.
- AI-agent demos without a real process owner.
- Revenue, conversion or time-saving claims without evidence.
- Content about automation for its own sake.
- Somebody else's demo or launch footage as the substance of a piece.
- A request for a comment, a word or a tag in exchange for a file or a link.

## The proof loop

`process teardown -> small internal test -> what broke -> revised workflow -> content -> next process`

We create our own evidence continuously. We do not wait for clients before building
proof, and we do not run out of internal evidence and become a news-reposting
account. Every Teardown should leave behind a candidate for the next Build, and
every Build should leave behind a failure worth its own Radar or Teardown.

## Cadence

Living section. Updated 8 September 2026 from the 1 September radar analysis.
Revised after every 12 publications and after each radar run.

Five publications a week:

- 2 M2 Radar
- 2 M2 Builds
- 1 M2 Teardown

Every second week, the fifth slot becomes a second Teardown in place of one Build,
so the mix alternates between 2 / 2 / 1 and 2 / 1 / 2. Teardowns never drop below
one a week: they are the format that feeds the next Build.

Each week should also produce one carousel drawn from the strongest Teardown of the
fortnight, and one workshop-channel message with the long version: the workflow
file, the prompts, and the part that did not work.

## Before anything is published

- Can the topic and the verdict be understood with the sound off?
- Is it recognizable as M2 with the handle hidden?
- Is the proof ours?
- Is a human review step named?
- Does the closing line give an action the viewer can take alone?
